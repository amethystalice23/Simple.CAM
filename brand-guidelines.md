# simple.cam brand guidelines

## Audience

Hobbyists who are new to CNC/CAM but come from a strong 3D-printing
background — highly technical makers, just new to *this* field. They
know firmware, slicers, and tuning; they don't yet know step-over or
why a 4th axis changes the feed words. Write for that reader: technical
respect, no assumed machining vocabulary.

## Tone & voice

- Plain-spoken, practical, warm.
- Second person ("you"), active voice.
- Short sentences — under 20 words as a rule.
- Contractions are fine and encouraged ("that's", "you'll").
- Precise and concrete: real numbers, real dimensions, real button
  names. If a sentence could survive in any manual, rewrite it.
- Not condescending: never explain what a file is, never say "don't
  worry". They are adults who can read a dial.
- Light personality is welcome; in-jokes and references that require
  project history are not.

## Vocabulary

**Always use:**
- Correct machining terminology, defined on first use and linked to the
  glossary (e.g. [stepover](docs/help/glossary.md#stepover)).
- 3D-printing analogies where they genuinely map, flagged as analogies
  ("a roughing pass is like infill: it removes the bulk").
- mm and degrees; Carvera names for machine features.

**Avoid:**
- Undefined jargon, in-jokes, and vague terms ("powerful", "flexible",
  "intuitive", "simply", "easy").
- Business-speak ("leverage", "synergy", "utilize").
- Hedging and filler ("note that", "it should be mentioned that").

## Voice sample

> An allowance is material deliberately left behind so a later (or
> finer) pass can remove it.

## Quick Reference

1. Plain-spoken, practical, warm. Second person, active voice.
2. Sentences under 20 words. Contractions OK.
3. Every machining term: define or glossary-link on first use.
4. No undefined jargon, in-jokes, vagueness, or business-speak.
5. Concrete numbers and real UI names over adjectives. Never talk down.
